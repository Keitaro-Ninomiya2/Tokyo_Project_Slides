\begin{frame}{Further analysis}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item How were promotions and bonus pay used to transfer or retain incumbent workers?

        \textbf{Findings:} Both were used more in impacted offices. Promotions were used more often together with transfers.

        \vspace{0.5em}

        \item How did early promotion help transferred workers in their subsequent careers?

        \textbf{Findings:}
        \begin{enumerate}
            \item Transferees sent to drafted offices outperform those sent to non-drafted offices.

            \item Transferees from remote teams perform worse than transferees from neighboring teams.
        \end{enumerate}
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Discussion and conclusion}
    \begin{itemize}[<+->]
        \item Workplaces compensated for staff losses jointly through internal and external markets (about 2:1 per drafted employee).

        \item These results support empirical work on responses to worker exits (Jaeger, Heining, and Lazarus, 2025; the worker-death paper).

        But we show that replacement costs varied with the composition of workers surrounding each affected worker.

        \item Transferred workers presumably accelerated promotion and contributed to better career trajectories.
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}
